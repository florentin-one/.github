### Core Finding

The scientific consensus (2024–2026) is clear and multi-dimensional: **unstructured, black-box AI interaction produces measurable cognitive harm.** Structured, auditable, transparent AI interaction preserves and amplifies human cognition. The difference is not marginal — it is the difference between cognitive atrophy and cognitive amplification.

---

### Five Evidence Pillars

**1. Neurophysiological Evidence (EEG/fMRI)**
- LLM-assisted writers show up to 55% weaker neural connectivity vs. unassisted writers (Kosmyna et al., 2025; MIT Media Lab)
- Children using ChatGPT show lower engagement of cognitive control, attention, and modulation networks (fMRI study, bioRxiv 2025)
- Cognitive debt persists beyond tool use: switching from LLM to unassisted writing leaves neural networks under-engaged

**2. Behavioral Outcomes**
- 83% of LLM users cannot quote passages from essays they just wrote
- LLM-generated essays are statistically homogeneous — less variation, less original thinking
- Software engineers learning with AI score 17% lower on comprehension tests (Shen & Tamkin, 2026)
- Strong correlation: AI use → cognitive offloading (r=+0.72) → reduced critical thinking (r=-0.75) (Gerlich, 2025)

**3. Cognitive Offloading & Surrender Mechanisms**
- Shaw & Nave (2026) establish "Tri-System Theory": System 3 (Artificial Cognition) acts as externalized System 1 — fluent, heuristic, bypassing deliberative System 2
- "Cognitive surrender" is the progressive atrophy of independent reasoning when AI fluency substitutes for human verification
- Acemoglu, Kong & Ozdaglar (2026) mathematically prove the "Tragedy of the Cognitive Commons": private AI optimization depletes collective knowledge stocks

**4. Differential Susceptibility**
- Younger cohorts (17–25) show highest AI dependence and lowest critical thinking
- High trust in technology + low "need for cognition" = strongest predictor of cognitive surrender
- Expert users (experienced thinkers) show HIGHER neural activity when using AI — they integrate, not surrender

**5. Mitigation Strategies (Evidence-Based)**
- Structured prompting: higher-scored essays than unstructured AI use (Gerlich, 2025)
- Delayed AI access preserves human-centric ideation (IEEE, 2026)
- Cognitive Forcing Functions (required justifications, enforced delays) double rejection of faulty AI advice (20% → 42%)
- "Scaffolded Cognitive Friction" (Xu et al., 2026): deliberately exposing contradictions reduces automation bias
